Odd-sector, Pontryagin-space and index-law extensions of the Connes–van Suijlekom real-zeros theorem

Mehmet Akif Oltulu · Maovera AI Research (independent), Istanbul, Türkiye
2026-10-18 · version 2.2 · doi:10.5281/zenodo.23459270

Full text (PDF) · Zenodo record

Abstract

Connes and van Suijlekom [1] proved that if the quadratic form attached to a real distribution on [0, L] defines a lower-bounded self-adjoint operator whose lowest eigenvalue is simple, isolated and carries an even eigenfunction ξ, then the Fourier transform ξ̂ has only real zeros. We resolve the two parity steps of their argument and obtain three extensions. Theorem 1: if a simple eigenvalue has κ eigenvalues below it, the transform of its eigenfunction has at most κ pairs of non-real zeros; for κ = 0 all zeros are real for odd as well as even ground states. Theorem 1 is the analogue, for the Connes–van Suijlekom class, of Gueguen’s theorem on eigenpolynomials of Hermitian Toeplitz matrices, and its two-sided form reproduces Gueguen’s count exactly. Theorem 2: the ground state of the odd sector alone has all its zeros on the real or the imaginary axis; positivity is required on the odd subspace only. Theorem 3: when exactly one eigenvalue lies below the odd ground state, the number of purely imaginary pairs (zero or one) is fixed by comparing the signs of the first moment and of the boundary behaviour. All results hold in finite dimension and, under the hypotheses of [1, Thm 6.1] together with a Rayleigh–Ritz lemma, at operator level. They are verified on random matrices of the class with negative controls and illustrated on truncated Weil quadratic forms.

Added in version 2.0. The zeros of the ground-state polynomial Pξ interlace with those of the companion Pβ⊙ξ built from the distribution itself (Theorem 4). For every simple eigenvalue λ an exact index law holds (Theorem 5): the number of pairs of non-real zeros of ξ̂ plus the number of real zeros of negative type—those where P′ξ Pβ⊙ξ > 0—equals the number κ(λ) of eigenvalues below λ, with two explicit terms added in the odd sector. Equivalently, Q − λ is congruent to minus the Bezout matrix of Pξ and Pβ⊙ξ (Corollary 5.1). For truncated Weil forms at q = 13, 29, 61 the law holds in all 237 eigenvectors with interval-certified signs, and all eigenvectors with eigenvalue below 10−3 are certified to have only real, simple zeros (Tables 3–4).

Added in version 2.1. A resolvent version of the index law (Theorem 6) reads the level of the spectrum from zeros: for x = (Q − µ)−1 e0 the number of pairs of non-real zeros of Px, plus its real zeros of negative type, plus [x0 < 0], equals the number of eigenvalues below µ. At µ = 0 this is a zero-location criterion for λ0 > 0. For the truncated Weil forms the criterion holds in all nine cells, and for matrices shifted to have 1, 2 or 4 negative eigenvalues it returns exactly these counts (Table 5).

Scope. Proved: Theorems 1–6 (finite dimension) and Theorems 1′–3′ (operator level). Computer-verified (interval arithmetic): zero certificates for truncated Weil forms (Tables 3–5). Measured: random-matrix verification (Table 1), Weil eigenvalue data (Table 2), excited-state observations (Section 15). Continuation: uniform isolation along windows (Section 1.1) and the capacity law of Section 15.
